\subsection{Browser User-Evaluation Metrics}

Let $N$ denote the number of unique URLs evaluated by users, $R$ the number of
false alarms explicitly reported by users, and $A$ the number of caution,
warning, or block decisions. The user-reported false-alarm rate is
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{URFAR}=\frac{R}{N}\times100.
\end{equation}
The overall alert rate is
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{OAR}=\frac{A}{N}\times100.
\end{equation}
Across the current-format exports, 5,302 unique URL observations were recorded,
users submitted 138 explicit false-alarm reports, and the extension produced
418 caution, warning, or block decisions. Therefore, URFAR was 2.60\% and OAR
was 7.88\%. One participant additionally confirmed 36 false alerts in
aggregate without stored URLs; including them gives a descriptive lower-bound
false-alarm burden of 3.28\%. URFAR is a
lower-bound descriptive rate because users may not report every mistake. OAR
measures the warning burden and is not a false-positive rate because some
alerts may be correct. Feedback completeness is established through participant
confirmation rather than by dividing reports by alerts. Older legacy exports
without a compatible unique-URL denominator are reported separately.
